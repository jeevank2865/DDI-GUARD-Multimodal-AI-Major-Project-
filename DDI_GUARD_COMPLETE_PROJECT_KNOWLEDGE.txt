1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

1.1 Title and abstract
Full title: DDI-GUARD: Multimodal Explainable AI for Drug–Drug Interaction Severity Prediction.

Abstract draft: Drug–drug interactions can alter therapeutic effects or increase patient risk, and pairwise interaction resources are difficult to search consistently at scale. DDI-GUARD is an academic research system that combines molecular graph features, drug–target network features, and drug-label text evidence to classify interactions into Minor, Moderate, and Major severity classes. Its data pipeline uses DDInter 2.0 labels, PubChem structures, ChEMBL 34 drug–target activity records, and DailyMed labeling passages. The implementation includes pair-disjoint and cold-drug evaluation, modality ablations, validation-fitted temperature calibration, evidence provenance, and a local research inference API. In the checked paired-support evaluation, the full model achieved macro-F1 0.7473 on 1,802 pair-disjoint test pairs and 0.3664 on 253 cold-drug test pairs. Cold-drug Minor recall was 0.1429 on 14 examples, illustrating substantial generalization limitations and class uncertainty. The system is a research demonstration; it has not been clinically validated and must not guide treatment.

1.2 Problem and motivation
DDI references often present heterogeneous descriptions and evidence; severity prediction is a multiclass learning problem with incomplete structures, target coverage, and label-text coverage. The project investigates whether complementary molecular, biological-network, and label-text signals can support research classification and evidence display. It does not claim to replace a drug label, pharmacist, clinician, or curated interaction database.

1.3 Objectives and contributions supported by artifacts
- Build a traceable ingestion and pair-label preparation workflow around DDInter 2.0.
- Construct molecular graph inputs from reviewed PubChem SMILES.
- Construct a relation-aware drug–target network from ChEMBL 34 activity records.
- Ingest DailyMed label sections with source and content hashes and produce split-specific TF-IDF features.
- Train and compare modality combinations on fixed pair-disjoint and cold-drug protocols, using common supports for paired comparisons.
- Fit scalar temperature calibration on validation data only for the selected run.
- Expose an explicitly research-only local inference path separately from the disabled production route.
- Show source provenance and missing-modality coverage where available.

These contributions describe engineering and experiments. They do not establish clinical benefit, causal mechanism, prospective validity, or regulatory status.

1.4 Intended users, boundaries, and status
Intended use: student/researcher demonstration, academic discussion, reproducible offline evaluation, and exploration of source evidence. Intended audience: B.Tech CSE evaluators and biomedical informatics/ML researchers. Boundaries: a dataset classification result is not a treatment recommendation; a confidence score is model output, not calibrated patient-level probability unless proven for that target deployment; LLM chat content is not model output or verified evidence.

2. BACKGROUND AND DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE

2.1 General scientific context (not a project finding)
A drug–drug interaction occurs when one medicine affects the exposure, effect, or safety profile of another. Pharmacokinetic interactions concern absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion; pharmacodynamic interactions concern effects at physiological targets or pathways. Severity depends on dose, route, duration, comorbidities, patient characteristics, and the specific evidence standard. A static pair classifier cannot model all those conditions unless it receives and validates them.

2.2 Dataset severity labels
The project maps source labels to Minor, Moderate, and Major according to DDInter records. “Minor” generally denotes lower clinical significance, “Moderate” a potentially clinically meaningful interaction that may require monitoring or adjustment, and “Major” a potentially serious interaction. These are broad source-category descriptions only; use DDInter's official documentation and the exact row-level source definition when writing a paper. This model predicts dataset categories, not patient-specific clinical severity.

2.3 Why the task is difficult
- Class imbalance: labeled DDInter rows are dominated by Moderate interactions.
- Pair overlap and drug familiarity can inflate random pair performance; pair-disjoint and cold-drug splits answer different questions.
- New drugs can lack molecular records, target-network edges, or DailyMed coverage.
- Label wording and source coverage are incomplete, heterogeneous, and potentially noisy.
- Biological relationships are not equivalent to clinical outcomes; graph edges are sourced activity records, not causal DDI labels.
- Cold-drug Minor support is only 14, so its class metrics are unstable.
- The model is trained and tested on curated data distributions that may not represent clinical practice.

2.4 Why evidence provenance matters
Every retrieved feature or explanatory text should retain source identity, record/document identifiers, retrieval time, and content hash. Provenance makes it possible to distinguish dataset labels from external support and generated language. The system's retrieved label passages are drug-level evidence. They are not necessarily a direct explanation of the specific pair's interaction.

4. DATA PREPROCESSING AND FEATURE ENGINEERING

4.1 Identity and pair processing
`ddi_guard/ingest.py` reads source CSVs, validates required fields, maps labels, canonicalizes unordered pairs by sorted DDInter identifiers, produces deterministic SHA-derived pair IDs, collapses duplicate labeled rows, and records exclusions/unknown labels. Drug identity mapping retains names and identifiers. Exact behavior is defined in code and manifests; do not substitute normalized names as a unique biomedical identity.

4.2 Molecular inputs
PubChem SMILES are validated and canonicalized/parsed with RDKit. Invalid or unresolved structures are tracked as missing. The graph builder creates atoms as nodes and bonds as edges, with 47 atom features and 16 bond features. Bond edges are represented bidirectionally. The architecture uses molecular graphs; 3D conformers are for UI visualization and are not classifier inputs. The feature construction code is in `ddi_guard/features.py` and related molecular preprocessing modules.

4.3 Target graph
ChEMBL activities are mapped to drug and target identifiers, quality filtered, and retained with target IDs, source record IDs, activity relation types, standardized values/units and provenance. A heterogeneous drug–target graph is converted into a relation-aware representation. It has 745 relation types in the audited graph. The global graph includes mapped entities beyond each supervised split, which creates a transductive external-side-feature caveat for cold-drug interpretation (Section 8).

4.4 Text inputs
DailyMed SPL passages are downloaded and retained with document/source provenance. The classifier's text modality is 2,048-dimensional TF-IDF, not PubMedBERT, a transformer embedding, or an LLM vector. The selected pair-disjoint text vectorizer is fit on training-drug documents only; validation/test documents are transformed without fitting. Text features are combined per drug pair symmetrically by sum, absolute difference, and elementwise product, giving 3×2048 pair-text input before its projection/encoder. Missing modalities are represented through explicit availability handling rather than evidence fabricated for missing drugs.

4.5 Splitting, normalization, labels, random state
Label order is `[Minor, Moderate, Major]`. Pair-disjoint protocol: seed 42, stratified 70/15/15, with 91,295 train, 19,563 validation, and 19,564 test pairs in the full labeled dataset. Cold-drug protocol partitions drug identifiers (1,331 train, 285 validation, 286 test), with 63,447 train, 3,093 validation, and 2,804 test pair candidates; 61,078 cross-partition pairs excluded. The full model has smaller common-support subsets (Section 8/9). Exact split manifest SHA-256: `b49366e1a39784f9f79c9652598357e531cf14f4f4b349d4feeaab8922c5773a`; dataset SHA-256: `e9c56c3ab38bb8746e825655e8875e99d6516dadb77dd38f60c29100acce33f`.

Do not assume all transforms are normalized: inspect the specific feature pipeline/config before stating normalization. Missing data may cause support filtering for a given modality combination. The model's explicit availability masks gate absent modalities; evaluation comparisons use matched support where specified.

5. ML MODELS AND ALGORITHMS

5.1 Molecular graph encoder
Custom molecular message-passing neural network implemented in `ddi_guard/model.py`, with three message-passing layers, hidden size 96, edge-conditioned MLP, residual updates, LayerNorm, and mean graph pooling. It is a molecular GNN. The inspected code does not establish that it is a canonical GIN or canonical PyG convolution; avoid naming it GIN. Atom and bond features are those described in Section 4.2.

5.2 Drug–target graph encoder
Custom relation-aware encoder with learned entity embeddings and relation-specific diagonal relation vectors, weighted neighbor aggregation normalized by absolute edge weight. It supports drug/target graph messages. It has R-GCN-like motivation, but inspection did not identify a canonical PyG RGCNConv implementation. Call it a custom relation-aware drug–target GNN; do not claim exact R-GCN unless source implementation is changed and verified.

5.3 Text/mechanism encoder
DailyMed passages transformed to sparse TF-IDF vectors of width 2,048, then pair represented symmetrically through concatenated sum, absolute difference, and product. Not PubMedBERT, BioBERT, transformer, or LLM. It is a text evidence feature pathway. It does not inherently extract a structured causal mechanism.

5.4 Fusion and classifier
Three modality representations are treated as tokens and passed through PyTorch MultiheadAttention with four heads, explicit availability masks, and a learned scalar gate followed by softmax weighting and weighted fusion. The classifier head is 96→96 with ReLU, dropout 0.15, then three output logits. Softmax yields the class probability vector. Attention/gate internals are not a validated explanation method.

5.5 Baselines and variants
- Modality ablations include text only, molecular+text, target+text, molecular+target control, and full molecular+target+text on matched supports (paired validation artifact).
- Random Forest baseline uses Morgan radius-2 fingerprints with 1,024 bits per drug and pair union/XOR/intersection (3,072 input bits); 150 trees, sqrt feature sampling, min leaf 2, balanced_subsample, seed 42. It is not the selected API model.
- Majority baseline appears in final audit for context.
- XGBoost: NOT FOUND as an implemented experiment in checked sources.
- 3D structure model: NOT IMPLEMENTED / NOT FOUND.
- Molecular atom attribution and target attribution: NOT IMPLEMENTED in verified inference path.

5.6 Checkpoints and inference
PyTorch checkpoints include model state/configuration and are loaded by the research adapter with compatibility checks. See Section 9 for selected path/hash. Checkpoint is a trained research artifact, not an approved clinical model. Do not change/overwrite checkpoint artifacts during document preparation.

6. MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION

Notation below describes verified code-level operations at a conceptual level. It is not a substitute for exact tensor-level code when preparing a methods paper.

6.1 Pair representation
For per-drug modality vector x_A and x_B, symmetric pair input is:
  p(x_A,x_B) = [x_A + x_B ; |x_A - x_B| ; x_A ⊙ x_B]
where `;` concatenates, `|.|` is elementwise absolute value, and `⊙` is elementwise product. This construction makes the pair representation invariant to swapping A and B. Source: pair feature construction in `ddi_guard/model.py`/feature pipeline; verify the exact call site before reproducing as a formal claim.

6.2 Molecular message passing
For atom node i at layer l, a generic notation matching the implemented edge-conditioned message passing is:
  m_i^(l) = AGG_{j∈N(i)} MLP_l(h_i^(l), h_j^(l), e_ij)
  h_i^(l+1) = LayerNorm(h_i^(l) + Update_l(m_i^(l)))
where h are node states, e_ij bond features, and aggregation is the implementation's neighbor aggregation. Three layers are used, hidden width 96, followed by mean pooling. Do not claim the canonical GIN update `MLP((1+ε)h_i + Σh_j)` unless code proves that exact form.

6.3 Target graph messages
A high-level form for the custom relation-aware layer is:
  m_i = normalize( Σ_{(j,r)∈E_i} w_{ijr} · (h_j ⊙ q_r) )
where q_r is a learned relation-specific diagonal vector and weights/normalization follow implementation. Entity embeddings are learned. This is descriptive of the custom layer; exact relation direction, sign, and normalization must be confirmed from current code before publishing equations.

6.4 Multimodal fusion and classification
Let z_m,z_t,z_x denote molecular, target, and text modality outputs and a indicate available modalities. Attention combines modality tokens under availability mask a. A learned scalar gate produces scores g_k; normalized weights are:
  α_k = exp(g_k) / Σ_{j∈available} exp(g_j)
  z = Σ_{k∈available} α_k · z_k
A classifier maps z through a 96-unit hidden layer, ReLU and dropout 0.15 to logits u∈R^3. Probabilities are `softmax(u)` (with temperature after fitting for the selected calibrated run). Missing modalities are masked, not replaced with invented feature content.

6.5 Objective and calibration
Training uses class-weighted cross-entropy with inverse training-class frequency weights and AdamW. Scalar temperature T is fit on validation logits by minimizing validation negative log-likelihood, optimizing log(T) with LBFGS. Calibrated probability is `softmax(u/T)`. For the selected pair-disjoint full model T=2.0856511593. Calibration is not clinical validation, and ECE/Brier metrics are distribution-dependent.

8. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN AND LEAKAGE PREVENTION

8.1 Pair-disjoint
Seed-42 stratified 70/15/15 split by canonical pair. Pairs are disjoint across train/validation/test; drugs can appear in multiple partitions. This measures generalization to unseen combinations among a largely familiar drug catalog, not unseen drugs.

8.2 Cold-drug
Drug identifiers are partitioned: 1,331 train, 285 validation, 286 test. Pairs crossing partitions are excluded (61,078 excluded). Labeled candidate split counts: train 63,447, validation 3,093, test 2,804. For full three-modality common support, test n=253, with Minor 14, Moderate 206, Major 33. Label-bearing test drugs are absent from supervised training/validation pairs under the cold-drug protocol.

Important qualification: the global ChEMBL graph and external feature records may include test-partition drugs. This is external-side-feature transduction, so call this a cold-drug label split with external features available. It is not feature-blind zero-shot learning. Audit whether unsupervised graph construction or text corpus processing has split-specific fitting for each reported experiment.

8.3 Manifests and paired support
Split manifest hash and dataset hash are given in Section 4. Paired ablation comparisons use the same test-pair IDs per split; this avoids support changes confounding macro-F1 differences. Other audit metrics can use different supports and must not be compared as if matched. DailyMed TF-IDF is trained on training-drug text only for the selected pair-disjoint run; calibration is fit on validation only.

8.4 Leakage/bias checks and risks
Canonical pair IDs avoid duplicate-pair leakage. Split manifests and pair overlap checks are recorded. Remaining concerns include shared drugs in pair-disjoint testing (by design), external graph features for cold drugs, data-source overlap, and historical DDInter/label-text information that could embed source-related signals. No patient-level independence or prospective site-based evaluation is established.

25. VIVA PREPARATION — 50 QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

Q1. What is DDI-GUARD?
A. An academic research system that classifies DDInter drug pairs into Minor, Moderate, or Major using molecular, target-network, and DailyMed text modalities, with local research inference and provenance display.

Q2. What problem does it address?
A. It explores computational prioritization of pairwise DDI severity labels. It does not replace clinical review or authoritative labels.

Q3. What are the three classes?
A. Minor, Moderate, and Major, mapped from DDInter labels. The model predicts dataset categories, not patient-specific risk.

Q4. What is a pharmacokinetic interaction?
A. An interaction affecting absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion. That is domain context; the model does not explicitly simulate all these processes.

Q5. What is a pharmacodynamic interaction?
A. An interaction between drug effects on biological targets or pathways. Model graph features are not proof of causal pharmacodynamics.

Q6. What is the main labeled dataset?
A. DDInter 2.0, ingested from eight ATC CSV exports listed in the audit.

Q7. How many unique labeled pairs were audited?
A. 130,422 after canonicalization and duplicate handling; experimental test supports are much smaller and must be stated separately.

Q8. Why canonicalize a pair?
A. The same unordered pair must have a single stable identity whether source order is A,B or B,A. The code sorts IDs and generates deterministic pair identifiers.

Q9. What happens to unknown labels?
A. They are tracked in the exclusion ledger; unknown-only pairs are not assigned a supervised severity label.

Q10. How are duplicate pair records handled?
A. Labeled duplicates are collapsed under the ingestion rules; the audit records 44,779 duplicate labeled rows and 62,148 duplicates including unknown rows.

Q11. What is a SMILES string?
A. A text representation of molecular structure; RDKit parses accepted SMILES into atom/bond graphs.

Q12. What molecular features are used?
A. 47 atom features and 16 bond features, with bidirectional bond edges as graph connectivity.

Q13. Is the model a GIN?
A. It is a custom edge-conditioned molecular message-passing GNN. A canonical GIN update was not verified, so I would not label it GIN.

Q14. What is message passing?
A. Neighbor information is transformed and aggregated to update node representations over graph layers.

Q15. How many molecular message-passing layers?
A. Three, with hidden size 96, residual updates, LayerNorm, and mean pooling per the inspected model.

Q16. What is ChEMBL used for?
A. ChEMBL 34 activity records provide drug-target relationships and provenance for a biological network feature pathway.

Q17. What is a target graph?
A. A graph linking drug entities and biological targets through sourced activity relations; graph presence is not itself a DDI label.

Q18. Is the target encoder a canonical R-GCN?
A. It is custom relation-aware with learned relation-specific diagonal vectors. It has R-GCN-like intent but canonical RGCNConv was not verified.

Q19. How is DailyMed used?
A. Selected SPL warning, drug-interaction, and clinical-pharmacology passages are retained and transformed into 2,048-dimensional TF-IDF vectors.

Q20. Is the text encoder PubMedBERT?
A. No. The checked implementation uses TF-IDF, not PubMedBERT or another transformer.

Q21. What does the pair representation do?
A. It concatenates sum, absolute difference, and elementwise product of drug feature vectors to treat the pair symmetrically.

Q22. What does multimodal mean here?
A. The model combines molecular graphs, a drug-target graph representation, and label text features.

Q23. How is fusion performed?
A. Modality outputs are processed as tokens with four-head attention, availability masks, and learned softmax gates for weighted fusion.

Q24. Does attention prove which modality caused a prediction?
A. No. Attention/gating values are internal weights and have not been validated as faithful explanations.

Q25. What is the classifier output?
A. Three logits converted to probabilities ordered Minor, Moderate, Major.

Q26. What loss is used?
A. Weighted multiclass cross-entropy, with inverse training class frequency weights in the reported full run.

Q27. How is the model optimized?
A. AdamW; the selected pair-disjoint full run uses learning rate 0.001 and weight decay 0.0001, with scheduler and gradient clipping as recorded.

Q28. How is checkpoint selected?
A. By validation performance with validation loss tie-break; selected pair-disjoint full checkpoint best epoch is 7, not chosen using test results.

Q29. What is temperature scaling?
A. Dividing logits by a scalar temperature before softmax. The scalar is fitted on validation NLL to adjust probability sharpness.

Q30. Why use macro-F1?
A. It averages class F1 equally, so minority classes influence the score more than in accuracy. It can still be unstable when a class has few examples.

Q31. What is the pair-disjoint split?
A. A stratified split over canonical pairs, where pairs do not repeat across partitions but the same drug may appear in multiple partitions.

Q32. What does cold-drug mean in this project?
A. Drugs are partitioned across train/validation/test and cross-partition pairs are excluded. It tests label generalization to held-out drug IDs, with external side-feature caveat.

Q33. Is cold-drug zero-shot feature-blind?
A. No. ChEMBL/global external features can include held-out drugs; describe it as cold-drug label evaluation with external features available.

Q34. Why is pair-disjoint performance higher?
A. The model can encounter familiar drugs and their features in different test pairs. That setting is easier than generalizing to unseen drug identities.

Q35. What is full-model pair-disjoint macro-F1?
A. 0.7473 on the common-support 1,802 test pairs in the paired ablation artifact.

Q36. What is full-model cold-drug macro-F1?
A. 0.3664 on 253 common-support pairs.

Q37. How many cold-drug Minor test cases are there?
A. Fourteen. Full-model Minor recall is 2/14, or about 0.1429, so this estimate is highly uncertain.

Q38. How did the full model perform on cold Major?
A. Major recall was 1/33 (0.0303); the confusion matrix shows 32 of 33 labeled Major were predicted Moderate.

Q39. Did the full model beat every ablation?
A. No. Molecular+Text had macro-F1 0.7836 on pair-disjoint common support, higher than full model's 0.7473.

Q40. What is the paired bootstrap?
A. A class-stratified resampling of the same test pair IDs, 10,000 replicates, seed 20261020, producing descriptive 95% confidence intervals for macro-F1 differences.

Q41. Why matched support?
A. If models are tested on different pairs due to missing modalities, score changes can reflect sample composition. Matched support isolates comparisons on the same examples.

Q42. What does ECE measure?
A. A binned difference between confidence and empirical accuracy. It depends on binning and data distribution and is not proof of clinical calibration.

Q43. What is the selected pair-disjoint checkpoint path?
A. `artifacts/multimodal_pair_disjoint_molecular_target_text_dailymed_common_support_trainfit_v1_support_molecular_target_text/best_validation.pt`, SHA-256 `9259c34a...d965c` (quote full hash from Section 9).

Q44. Is prediction enabled in production?
A. No. `/api/predict` remains HTTP 503 because approval metadata has approved=false and no approved checkpoint; `/api/research/predict` is a separate local academic route.

Q45. Does the research API make the model clinically ready?
A. No. It provides local demonstration inference only and does not satisfy clinical validation or regulatory approval.

Q46. What is the difference between evidence and explanation?
A. Evidence is retrieved source material with provenance. An explanation attributes a model output; this project has not verified atom-level or causal explanations.

Q47. Are 3D conformers model inputs?
A. No. The classifier uses molecular graph features; 3D structures are for visualization.

Q48. How does the assistant differ from the DDI model?
A. The assistant generates language through an external AI provider. The prediction API runs the trained model; an assistant answer is not a model probability or verified mechanism.

Q49. Does the project support Gemini?
A. No Gemini SDK/provider was found in the checked source. Current code reportedly supports OpenAI/OpenAI-compatible provider configuration; verify current env settings before demo.

Q50. What is the most important limitation to state?
A. The system is not clinically validated, cold-drug performance is weak with very low Minor/Major recall, and DailyMed coverage is partial. It must not guide treatment.

26. GLOSSARY

DDI — Drug–drug interaction: one drug may alter the effect/exposure/safety of another.
GNN — Graph neural network: a neural model that updates node/graph representations using graph neighborhoods.
GIN — Graph Isomorphism Network: a specific GNN family; canonical GIN was not established as this project's layer.
R-GCN — Relational graph convolutional network: graph convolution supporting multiple edge relations; the project uses a custom relation-aware encoder, not verified canonical R-GCN.
SMILES — String representation of a molecular structure.
Molecular graph — Atoms as nodes and bonds as edges.
Multimodal fusion — Combining signals from different modalities; here molecular, target graph, text.
Attention — Learned weighting/mixing over representations; not automatically an explanation.
Calibration — Agreement between predicted probability and empirical frequency under a defined distribution.
Macro-F1 — Arithmetic mean of class-wise F1 scores.
AUROC — Area under receiver operating characteristic curve; multiclass results depend on averaging strategy.
Brier score — Mean squared error of probabilistic predictions against one-hot outcomes.
ECE — Expected calibration error, typically a bin-weighted confidence/accuracy gap.
Pair-disjoint split — Pair identities are separated between partitions; drugs may overlap.
Cold-drug split — Drug identities are partitioned to evaluate label generalization on held-out drugs; feature access must be explicitly described.
Inference — Applying a trained model to input features to obtain outputs.
Checkpoint — Saved model state/configuration used for later inference or evaluation.
Provenance — Metadata tracing a record/feature to its source, identifier, timestamp and content hash.
TF-IDF — Term frequency–inverse document frequency text vectorization.
SPL — Structured Product Label, the structured label content distributed through DailyMed.
Temperature scaling — Validation-fitted scalar transformation of logits for probability calibration.
